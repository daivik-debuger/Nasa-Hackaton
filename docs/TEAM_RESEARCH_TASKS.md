# Three-person research plan

The project lead owns AI, software, integration, product design, and the final pitch. These assignments are evidence work only. A folder of links or an AI-written essay is not a deliverable.

## Shared submission requirements

Every finding must include a direct source, author/organization, year, page/section, region, method, limitation, confidence, and allowed product use. Submit original PDFs where redistribution is permitted; otherwise submit DOI/official links and reading notes. Record disagreements rather than averaging them away.

## Researcher 1: NASA and climate-data audit

Research NASA POWER, GPM IMERG, SMAP, MODIS vegetation indices, relevant Landsat products, and NEX-GDDP-CMIP6.

For each, document current version, variables, units, spatial/temporal resolution, coverage, available years, latency, method (observed/modeled/derived/reanalysis), access/API, authentication, quality flags, missing values, attribution, agricultural uses, and limitations.

Minimum literature:

- Three agriculture evaluations of NASA POWER.
- Two satellite-rainfall versus gauge studies.
- Two agricultural SMAP studies.
- Two MODIS vegetation-monitoring studies.
- One field-scale remote-sensing limitations study.
- One climate-scenario/crop-planning study.

Deliver: NASA evidence matrix, source library, safe/prohibited claims, and recommendation for the two MVP datasets.

## Researcher 2: rotation and soil-health evidence

Research rotation effects on soil carbon/organic matter, nitrogen, water infiltration/use, erosion, structure, biology, weeds, pests, disease, yield, stability, drought resilience, cover crops, and economics.

Minimum literature:

- Three systematic reviews/meta-analyses on rotation.
- Three soil-health studies.
- Two drought studies.
- Two diversity/yield-stability studies.
- Two legume/nitrogen studies.
- Two cover-crop studies.
- One failure/disadvantage study.
- One economics study.

For each finding capture crops, region, soil/climate, management, duration, sample size, result, significance, benefits, counter-effects, and applicability.

Deliver: evidence matrix, reviewed crop/rotation facts, ten strongest findings, ten important risks, safe rules, prohibited rules, and source library.

## Researcher 3: pilot region, farmer needs, and competitors

Compare three candidate pilot regions using crops/rotations, climate hazards, soil issues and data access, published agricultural research, mobile/internet context, and the team's ability to explain the region. Recommend one using a transparent scoring table.

Find at least ten published farmer surveys/reports covering rotation decisions, adoption barriers, weather information, digital trust, costs/equipment, connectivity, climate risk, recommendation rejection, and data-entry willingness.

Test/research at least ten existing farm, crop-planning, weather, soil, rotation, or climate-risk tools. Record audience, location, price, data sources, features, offline/mobile support, strengths, weaknesses, transparency, and the specific gap FieldShift addresses.

Deliver: region comparison, farmer-needs evidence matrix, competitor matrix, recommended pilot region, five evidenced needs, five competitor gaps, and a defensible FieldShift value statement.

## Acceptance

Research is complete only when another team member can open the cited source and reproduce every major conclusion. Direct farmer interviews remain future work and must not be implied.
